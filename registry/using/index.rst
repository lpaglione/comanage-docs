Registry Features and Use
==========================

Registry's capabilities are organized into seven areas. Each section below
describes what that area of Registry does and why it matters, inventories the
features available within it, and links to pages covering configuration details,
technical references, recipes, and case studies.

.. grid:: 2
   :gutter: 3

   .. grid-item-card:: People
      :link: people
      :link-type: doc

      Managing Person records, identities, attributes, authenticators, and
      the identity lifecycle — the core of what Registry does.

   .. grid-item-card:: Structure
      :link: structure
      :link-type: doc

      Modeling your population's structure with Collaborative Organizations
      (COs), Collaborative Organizational Units (COUs), and Groups.

   .. grid-item-card:: Lifecycle Management
      :link: lifecycle
      :link-type: doc

      Enrollment Flows, Pipelines, and Offboarding — bringing people into
      Registry, keeping their records current, and managing the end of their
      relationship with your organization.

   .. grid-item-card:: External Connections
      :link: external-connections
      :link-type: doc

      Connecting Registry to the world: identity sources that bring data in,
      provisioning that pushes data out, authenticators, and identifier
      assignment.

   .. grid-item-card:: Operations
      :link: operations
      :link-type: doc

      Background jobs, history records, notifications, and the tools for
      keeping your Registry instance running smoothly.

   .. grid-item-card:: Personalization
      :link: personalization
      :link-type: doc

      Adapting Registry to your organization: dashboards, message templates,
      attribute enumerations, localization, and theming.

   .. grid-item-card:: Extending Registry
      :link: extending
      :link-type: doc

      An overview of the Registry plugin architecture and how plugins extend
      the platform's capabilities.
